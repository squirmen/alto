V5.2 · Joining records to their crowns

10 October 2026. This release joins Council, OpenStreetMap, notable-tree and kauri-survey records that V5.1 left unlinked to the crowns they stand on. Where no V5 crown covered a recorded tree, it draws one from the 2024 laser returns at the record’s position. V5 crown boundaries are not redrawn, and no record, source position or survey history is removed.

What changed

3,088 of the 37,409 records V5.1 left unlinked are now joined to a crown: 1,043 linked and 1,456 probable matches to existing V5 crowns, and 589 on 534 crowns drawn from the record.

  • 1,655 records joined 1,456 crowns whose map marker was a LiDAR detection. One record per crown now represents it, drawn where the crown’s marker was; the detection stays in the crown’s list of records.
  • 844 records joined 664 crowns that already had a source record as their marker. They are listed in that tree’s profile and do not get a second marker. The one exception: a protected or notable tree never loses its marker. On 1 crown a protected record now represents the crown in place of an ordinary record, which stays in the crown’s list; 14 protected records whose crown is already represented by another protected tree keep their own marker where their source recorded them, and the crown’s estimates are still counted once.
  • 457 crowns drawn from a record are 3 m or taller. They are drawn faintly, counted in the totals and service estimates with a flag, and await confirmation. The other 77 are 2–3 m tall and follow the existing low-canopy rule: shown, but not counted.

As a result the map shows 2,540 fewer separate markers (3,574,825 in all), 457 more counted crowns, and the annual value estimate rises by NZ$47,218 (record-seeded crowns NZ$15,926; crowns now represented by a source record, recalculated for its recorded taxon, NZ$31,291). The count of protected trees on the map is 3,570 as in V5.1.

Four further crowns drawn from a record were left out because each enclosed a small existing V5 crown; their records stay unlinked, unchanged on the map, and in review.

How records were joined

For each unlinked record, nearby crowns were scored on the distance from the record to the crown’s edge, weighed with an error model learned by comparing the crowns around unlinked records with those around decoy points nearby; on whether the crown’s height and area suit the recorded taxon; on conflicts with other records’ taxa on the same crown; and on how many records a crown of that size usually carries. Neighbouring records were assigned together, so a small crown cannot take several records when one has its own. A record was linked only when its crown was clearly better than every alternative, including no crown at all. Shifting whole digitised rows of records was tested and is not used.

Where tree-height laser returns stand at a record and no V5 crown covers them, a crown was drawn with V5’s own recipe, including the unclassified returns of palms V5 could not see where the imagery is green.

The hold-out hides 31,119 trusted links, moves each record as far from its crown as unlinked records of its source typically are, adds copies whose crown is deleted, and finds them again (0.919 for linked and probable when every unlinked record without a crown is assumed crown-missing). The audit is a blind visual judgement of fresh records under the final rules, from 2024 imagery, near-infrared and laser heights. Cards that could not be decided are left out; counting them as wrong gives floors of 0.76, 0.51 and 0.69. The hold-out cannot measure drawn crowns, so their figure comes from the audit alone.

Council recorded positions

The Council recorded positions switch in Layers (off by default) shows, for every joined record, where its source recorded the tree and a dashed line to the top of the crown it was joined to. The record’s own position is unchanged in its full record; the map marker sits on the crown.

What went to review

20,704 records went to the researcher workbench and appear on the map as before: 14,760 with competing crowns to choose between, 4,783 with nothing of tree height within 3 m, 1,021 on tree-height returns no crown could be drawn on, and 140 whose position cannot be matched (for example on a roof). The workbench also asks for confirmation of every crown drawn from a record.

What stays unlinked

13,613 records lie where there are no V5 crowns and no 2024 point cloud: 12,925 kauri observations in the Waitākere Ranges and other forest, 687 notable trees and one OpenStreetMap tree. Their full records say so. “Crown unlinked” does not mean a tree is absent.

What a link means

A link is spatial evidence that a record describes the tree on that crown. It does not prove that every record on a shared canopy is the same organism, and a drawn crown is a proposal until it is confirmed.
