This release gives every tree on the map a permanent number, publishes a table that resolves every tree ID and crown key ALTO has published, and applies the corrections ledger in full. Crowns, measurements and estimates are those of release 6 except on the trees a correction changed.

What changed

  • 4,200,336 map trees have a permanent tree number, such as TMCTT6X4. Each tree’s permanent link is https://alto.tfwelch.com/tree/ followed by its number.1
  • An alias table of 19,376,165 keys resolves every tree number, record ID and crown key published since June 2026 to the tree that carries it now, or to the reason it carries none.2
  • The corrections ledger is applied in full: 14 corrections change the map and the tree records, 17 are recorded with their trees, 318 were already true and 5 await review; none is pending.3
  • The map shows 3,574,821 trees (3 fewer than release 6) and counts 3,523,021 crowns (3 fewer); 3,570 trees are protected, as in release 6.

Tree numbers

A number belongs to a tree as the map draws it: one number for each map marker after the corrections. A crown’s number covers its marker, its outline and every record on the crown.

Of the 6,238,420 records, 4,200,336 are a tree’s marker, 2,038,080 sit on another record’s crown and carry that tree’s number, and 4 were merged into another tree by a reviewer and carry the surviving tree’s number. Each full record lists its tree’s number, its role (marker, member or merged) and the release that issued the number.

Numbers are drawn at random from about 1.07 billion possible numbers and checked against every number issued before; a number is never reused and encodes nothing about the tree. The record ID (akl_tree_…) stays the key of each full record.

Later releases carry numbers over. A crown that continues keeps its number: at least half of it lies in one new crown and no other new crown holds a fifth. When several numbered trees become one, the number with outside references, corrections or a source record survives (otherwise the largest overlap) and the others are marked merged. A split keeps the number on the part holding the representative record and lists every part. A tree with no successor is retired with a reason and its last position. A change of representative alone never changes a number.4

Format and check character

A tree number is T followed by 6 characters and 1 check character, from the 32 characters 0–9 and A–Z without I, L, O and U (Crockford’s base 32). The check character is a Luhn mod 32 checksum of the 6 characters after T.

  • Input: any case, with spaces or a hyphen (tmct-t6x4); I and L read as 1, O as 0.
  • Every single mistyped character fails the check; so do 99.8% of swapped neighbouring characters.
  • A number that fails its check is refused. The map’s search and the tree page offer a correction when exactly one number one keystroke away exists in the release.

Older IDs

The alias table resolves each key to a tree number and the record that shows the tree. The tree page (/tree/, tree.html?id=) and the map (?tree=, the search box) read it for every ID. An ID of a current tree opens that tree with one line naming its number. A retired, merged or split ID opens a page stating its status and reason, with links to the trees that took its place.

What stays the same

Every release 6 record, link, marker, source position, survey history, estimate and growth-form field is kept in the release database; record IDs do not change. Outside the corrected trees, the map tiles equal release 6’s apart from the number on each point and crown.

Limits

  • A number names a tree as mapped in this release. When a later release redraws crowns, a number can be retired, merged or split under the carry-over rules; its page then says which.
  • Reviewer outlines change the crown area; the height stays the release 5.2 measurement of the crown drawn from the record.
  • The near-canopy layer still draws the low-canopy polygon under the crown promoted for C00307.
  • KYTE takes record IDs only, so its links from ALTO carry the record ID; KYTE still files the C00002 visit under the notable record.
  • Outside the alias table: near-canopy (low and sub-canopy) polygon keys other than the promoted one, KYTE’s own visit IDs, 772 link-review candidate crowns and 400 notable-tree IDs reserved but never published.
  • Five corrections await review and are not applied.
  • Benefit and trunk estimates are not independently validated; they remain sensitivity scenarios.
