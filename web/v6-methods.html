This release estimates a growth form for each crown of the 2024 LiDAR survey: whether it looks like an evergreen or a deciduous broadleaf tree, a conifer, or a palm, cabbage tree or tree fern. It redraws every crown with a smoothed outline of the same cells, revises the benefit estimates with the growth form, and applies reviewer corrections from a ledger. Every growth form is a model estimate, never an identified species: where a source records a species, that name stays the tree’s species.

What changed

  • 3,352,099 crowns on 3,101 survey tiles were scored for growth form. 2,771,551 (82.7%) show one; 580,548 (17.3%) read growth form not yet determined.1
  • A blind audit of the scores on 12 October 2026 checked the calls before publication. Two kinds of call that fell short are withheld and one is reworded; shown calls were right for 0.921 of crowns after those changes (95% interval 0.848–0.980).
  • Every crown is drawn with a smoothed outline of its release 5 cells: 3,352,099 release 5 crowns and 534 crowns drawn from a record in release 5.2. Crown area, height and every estimate keep the cell area.
  • Benefit estimates follow the growth form where the release had no recorded species. Estimated annual benefits rise by NZ$3,186,004 a year; stored carbon falls by 114,533 t CO₂e and avoided runoff by 483,669 m³ a year.
  • 354 reviewer corrections from the ledger are accounted for, one of them applied to the map: a notable tree now stands on the crown its plaque marks.

Growth form

A classifier reads each crown’s structure in the 2024 LiDAR returns, its surroundings and image features from the 2024 aerial photograph at the crown top. It was trained on about 22,000 crowns, 1,600 of them outside the central isthmus: Council tree register records, plus crowns labelled from the 2024 aerial imagery and LiDAR profiles by a machine-learning image-interpretation model (one pass; no independent second labeller yet). It was checked on 600 fresh crowns, labelled the same way, that it never saw. A separate palm detector confirms palms it can see from above. Crowns flown on 30 April, 4 July and 21 October, when deciduous trees are in leaf, are scored by a variant trained for leaves on the trees (417,335 crowns).2

A growth form is shown only where calls of its kind were right often enough when tested on labelled crowns: at least 8 in 10 for a firm call and at least 6 in 10 for a “possibly” call. Otherwise the crown reads “growth form not yet determined”. After the audit no “possibly” call is shown in this release. On the leaf-on flights only evergreen broadleaf calls and palms seen by the palm detector are shown. The map and the tree page mark every growth form as an estimate, and a species recorded by the Council or another source stays the species shown, with the growth form beside it.

The 12 October audit

Before publication, about 200 crowns drawn from what the release would show, stratified by growth form, area and flight season, were labelled the same way, by the machine-learning image-interpretation model, together with abstentions and decoys, blind to the classifier’s calls, from the 2024 aerial imagery (7.5 cm) and LiDAR profiles: one pass, no independent second labeller yet. The labels were hashed before the key was opened, and the pass rules were written before any label existed. Crowns used to train, tune or test the model were left out.3

Shown calls were right for 0.899 of crowns (95% interval 0.828–0.956, weighted to the metro; 186 of 214 decided crowns), against a bar of 0.85. Two kinds of call failed or nearly failed their own bar and are withheld; one wording was changed:

Smoothed outlines

Release 5 traced crowns along the 0.5 m cells of the LiDAR survey, so they looked square. Release 6 draws each crown with a smooth outline around the same cells. The crown’s area, height and every estimate keep the cell area; the outline’s own area is stored beside it as the crown outline area. Outlines keep 0.995 of the cell area at the median (1st–99th percentile 0.939–1.019; 0.997 in total). Crowns drawn from a record in release 5.2 are smoothed the same way.

The property pages count canopy from the redrawn outlines, so the map and a property agree: canopy over parcels is 185.65 km², 0.34% less than from the cells (186.28 km²). Smoothing lets neighbouring outlines touch: overlapping neighbours share 10,832 m² in all, 0.005% of the counted canopy, which the property totals can count twice.

Benefit estimates

Where a crown has no recorded species, its benefit estimates now follow the growth form. For a shown call, leaf area and the curve from height to trunk diameter are averaged over the four growth-form classes, weighted by the model’s probabilities; every other class parameter (rain interception, biomass, root-to-shoot ratio, carbon fraction) follows the shown call, as release 5 let its class drive every parameter. A crown whose growth form is not yet determined takes the evergreen broadleaf default, as release 5 did; an older image-model class, uncalibrated, is replaced by that default. Recorded species keep their estimates.4

Most of the rise in value comes from crowns the older image model had called conifers: 498,012 of them are now evergreen broadleaf. Most of the fall in stored carbon comes from crowns now called conifers (−301,504 t CO₂e), 111,116 of which release 5.2 valued as evergreen broadleaf trees: for the same height the conifer curve gives a thinner trunk and less biomass.

Other readings of the rule were tested: keeping the release 5.2 class for the parameters the rule does not name would add NZ$4,979,744 a year, averaging every estimate over the classes NZ$2,578,258, and treating every palm-detector crown as a palm NZ$3,134,091. The estimates remain sensitivity scenarios.

Trunk diameter

Trunk diameter (DBH) is the diameter at 1.3 m above the uphill ground. No trunk in this release was measured. Each crown holds two model estimates: one from crown size and height, unchanged from release 5, and one from the benefit equations, which from this release follows the growth form where no species is recorded (its height-to-diameter curve is weighted as described under Benefit estimates). The tree panel and tree page show their median as the central estimate, marked Estimate, with the two models as its range. A trunk measured with a tape at 1.3 m at a reviewed KYTE ground visit replaces both. Tree ferns take the palm curve until a tree-fern value is chosen.

The root protection radius on the tree page is 12 times the central trunk estimate, its area capped at 707 m² (a 15 m radius): a planning scenario, not a measured root extent.

Corrections ledger

Reviewer decisions about trees, crowns and records are kept in an append-only ledger. After the build, each decision is applied to the release or recorded with the trees it names, and the release publishes a receipt of every outcome. Each tree page lists the decisions that name the tree, its records or its crown.5

What stays the same

Every release 5.2 record, link, marker, source position, survey history, review decision and accepted KYTE visit is kept, with the release 5 growth-form fields and service rows, in the release database. Crown boundaries and sizes are those of release 5 and 5.2. The map shows 3,570 protected trees and counts 3,523,024 crowns, as in release 5.2. Low and sub-canopy vegetation and the Council recorded positions are unchanged.

Limits

  • A growth form is a coarse class, not a species. Shown calls were about 94% right on the 600-crown blind test outside the isthmus (design-weighted) and 0.92 on the audit; the conifer and palm-type figures rest on small samples.
  • The labels made from imagery, used in training, in the blind tests and in the audit, come from a machine-learning image-interpretation model in one pass, from aerial imagery and LiDAR profiles; no independent second labeller has checked them, so agreement between labellers is not measured.
  • “Likely conifer” outside the isthmus is mostly tall forest crowns, 21–38 m, where pine, eucalypt and podocarp cannot be told apart from above. The audit could neither confirm nor reject its 0.80 bar.
  • Tree ferns use the palm leaf area and trunk curve until a tree-fern value is chosen.
  • Crowns drawn from a record in release 5.2, crowns outside the release 5 survey footprint and records without a crown have no growth form.
  • Outlines smooth the release 5 cells; crowns over 60 m² remain about 20% smaller than the reference outlines they were tested against. Low and sub-canopy polygons are not smoothed.
  • Benefit and trunk estimates are not independently validated; they remain sensitivity scenarios.
